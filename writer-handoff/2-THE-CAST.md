# 2 — The Cast

Every character has a color. It's their accent in the game — portrait
border, nameplate, their whole visual signature. Worth knowing because
it's the fastest shorthand for who you're writing.

| | Character | Color | Where |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 🟣 | **THERAPIST** | Violet `#9370db` | A voice on the phone |
| 🟡 | **DEBORAH** | Gold `#ffd700` | Her condo, third floor |
| 🟦 | **RWANDA** | Teal `#00ced1` | The alley behind the bar |
| 🔵 | **SAMUN** | Royal blue `#4169e1` | The gas station, night shift |
| 🔴 | **RICK** | Brick red `#b22222` | The biker bar |

## 🟣 THERAPIST — the tutorial

**Never seen.** A voice through the app on your phone. Tired but warm,
overworked, genuinely listening. You can hear a chart open on her end and
another call already waiting.

She teaches the whole game without explaining any of it — you learn what
swiping left does by feeling how she takes it. **Never let her explain a
mechanic.**

Her one exchange: *"What happened in the dream?"* The truth costs you but
gets her guard down. The lie is barely a lie and costs almost nothing —
it's the player's first swipe and it's meant to be gentle.

---

## 🟡 DEBORAH — grief, sealed shut

A dingy condo, half rented out, half prayer closet.

**What she says:** God took her son when He needed him.
**What's true:** she needs that to be true more than she needs it to be
kind.

She's devout, brittle, and smiles too wide when you get close. Push and
she flinches, then — maybe — nods, barely, and it costs her. Agree with
her and she hardens another layer and thanks you for it. Agreeing is the
single most expensive lie in the chapter.

**Voice:** she speaks in the language of faith, not the language of
therapy. *"God took my boy when He needed him"* — never *"faith is how I
process grief."*

---

## 🟦 RWANDA — tired of being made digestible

Posted by the takeout window, neon overhead, sequins.

**What she says:** *"Why do I gotta sand myself down just to be
digestible?"*
**What's true:** exactly that. She's not confused about her situation.
She's watching to see whether you'll be honest about it.

Queer, femme, an artist. Direct. She clocks a placating line instantly
and goes flat when she hears one. Every lie available with her is a small
social-lubrication lie — *"it's not that bad here"* — the kind that costs
you nothing to say and lands like a door closing.

She's also the only one who gets genuinely warm when you're honest:
*"Okay. You're alright."*

---

## 🔵 SAMUN — deflecting, and grateful when caught

Gas station, graveyard shift, wiping his hands on a rag that stopped
being clean a long time ago.

**What he says:** *"Everyone's hooked on something. Mine's just more
honest."*
**What's true:** he wants the pain to stop and he's built a whole
argument for why that's fine.

Jokes as armor. Visibly relieved when you play along, visibly caught when
you don't. The important thing about his truth path: **it doesn't fix
anything.** He looks at you — *really* looks — and that's all. Don't
write him a rescue.

---

## 🔴 RICK — proving something to someone who isn't there

The biker bar, back to the wall, blood on his knuckles that isn't his.

**What he says:** a guy looked at him wrong, and now that guy's jaw is
broken, and that's just how it works out here.
**What's true:** he's terrified he isn't a real man unless somebody
bleeds for him.

Terse, defensive, performing hard enough to drown out the question
underneath. Agree with him and he locks in permanently. Name it and
something cracks in his face — he almost answers. Then he doesn't.

**One special thing:** if the player has burned enough trust before
reaching him, Rick won't talk to them at all. *"Word gets around. I know
what you are."* Door stays shut. He's the only character who can lock the
player out.

## Also in the chapter

**BOB BAITER** — the councilman in the opening ad. Not someone you talk
to. He delivers the lake's official story straight to camera, cheerfully,
including the parts that contradict it. He's the game's first lie, told
to the player before they've made a single choice of their own.
