# 1 — The Game

## The pitch

**Dream Xtreme** is a short story game played on a phone, one swipe at a
time. **Swipe left to tell the truth. Swipe right to tell a lie.**

**Chapter 1: Truth Debt — Lake Ulysses.** You wake up on the shoulder of
a road by a lake, ears ringing, clothes damp, with a half-remembered
dream. You spend a night meeting four people, each holding onto a lie
that's keeping them upright. Every comforting lie you tell calms the
moment down and adds to a hidden debt. Every truth cracks something open
right now instead of later.

Then the debt comes due.

## The world

Lake Ulysses is a real toxic lake with a civic PR problem. "Three
thousand acres. A jewel," say the signs — and every few months it closes
again, algae bloom, "under control," reopens, closes. The town has an
official story and a real one, and everybody in it has picked which one
they live in.

The lake is a character. It gets visibly worse as your debt climbs.

The game's very first scene is a councilman cheerfully announcing the
lake is safe while listing the things that will happen to you if you
touch the water. That's the tone: deadpan official dishonesty sitting
right next to real grief, and neither one played for a joke.

## The shape of the chapter

```
OPENING          Quote → the councilman's ad → you wake up →
                 3 questions → your therapist calls

THEN, 4 TIMES    Walk to their door → meet them, pick how you
                 open → the conversation
                 (Deborah, then Rwanda, then Samun, then Rick)

ENDING           Reckoning — face your biggest lies again →
                 one of four endings
```

## What the player actually does

**Picks a feeling.** Before answering, they choose an emotion off a
wheel. It colors the moment and nudges the numbers. There's no correct
one.

**Swipes.** Left is the truth, right is the lie. That's the whole verb.

**Reads.** Text draws letter by letter, old-JRPG style. Tap once to
finish the line, tap again to move on. Nothing rushes them.

**Walks.** Between characters they walk to the next door and tap things
to look at them. They can't fail, can't die, can't get stuck. It's
atmosphere and pacing, not a test.

## The numbers

Four meters the player can see — **Integrity, Trust, Stability,
Lucidity** — plus one that matters more than the rest:

**Truth Debt.** Lies add to it. Truths don't. It's shown as a small
pulsing mark that gets more agitated as it climbs. Hit the top and the
game cuts straight to the ending, wherever you are.

Every lie worth remembering also gets written down in a **ledger**. At
the end the game reads them back to you one at a time and asks: confess,
or double down?

Final debt picks the ending:

| Debt | Ending |
| :-- | :-- |
| Low | **Clean Cut** — the lake closes, the water clears, nobody thanks you |
| Mid | **Functional Mask** — reopened, carefully worded, a few dead fish |
| High | **Collapse** — sirens at dusk, nobody in charge |
| Max | **Living Lie** — reopened and photogenic, dead fish edited out |

## One thing that affects your writing

The player is secretly sorted into one of three types — **Guns**,
**Bible**, or **Crystals** — by three questions at the start. They're
never told. It decides which feelings are on their wheel, and it means
**some lines need three versions**: the same room or moment seen three
ways.

| Type | Reads the world as |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Guns** | Threat, exits, who's dangerous. Blunt. |
| **Bible** | Who's still trying. Who deserves better. Moral weight. |
| **Crystals** | Atmosphere, dread, what a place feels like. |

You'll see this in `script-extra/3-rooms.txt`, where every line has a
Guns, Bible and Crystals version. Same fact, three readings — not three
different plots.
